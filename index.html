This project demonstrates that when future information is unavailable and decisions cannot be reversed, optimal decision-making shifts from exact optimization toward strategies that balance learning and action. Remarkably, simple threshold-based rules can achieve provably optimal or near-optimal performance, highlighting the power of principled heuristics in uncertain environments.

Infrared detector based on colloidal quantum dots.

Project Repository / Report

This project aims to develop an alternative infrared detector based on colloidal quatum dots (CQDs) made of mercury telluride (HgTe). These semicondutor nanoparticles have a remarkable property: their absorption wavelength can be adjusted simply by controlling their size during synthesis. The larger the dots, the more they absorb low-energy photons, and therefore longer wavelengths. This flexibility paves the way for infrared detectors that could be less expensive and easier to manufacture than conventional technologies.
